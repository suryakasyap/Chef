There's a reason one specific smell can put you right back in your mother's kitchen, ten years ago, without warning.

It's sometimes called the Proust effect, after the writer who described a memory flooding back the moment he tasted a madeleine dipped in tea.

Smell is processed by the olfactory bulb, which sits unusually close to the brain's memory and emotion centers — closer than any other sense gets.

That's why a smell can trigger a memory faster and more vividly than a photo ever does. You don't remember the kitchen — you're suddenly just back in it.

For most of us in the survey, this wasn't abstract. It was oddly specific: one person's answer was literally "the smell of ghee on rice, my grandmother's house, age six."
