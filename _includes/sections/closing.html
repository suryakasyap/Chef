Going into this project, comfort food felt like a fairly light topic — something fun to survey, not something to take too seriously. That changed pretty quickly.

What we found is that comfort eating isn't a habit to fix — it's a working coping mechanism, built on real neuroscience, shaped by culture, and increasingly targeted by marketing that understands it better than most of us do.

Where this could go next: a longer-term study tracking the same people's comfort food choices across a full semester, to see how triggers shift with actual stress cycles like exams — something we didn't have time for here, but would genuinely want to see.
