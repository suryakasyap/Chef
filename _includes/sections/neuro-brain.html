We kept hearing "dopamine hit" thrown around without really knowing what it meant, so we looked into it properly. Here's the short version.

{% include picture.html img="ramen-overhead" class="figure--wide" %}

When you eat something you associate with comfort, your brain doesn't just register taste — it releases dopamine through the same reward circuit involved in most pleasurable experiences.

That's the part everyone knows. What we didn't expect is that the anticipation matters almost as much as the eating itself — the dopamine spike often peaks before the first bite.

Serotonin plays a slower, quieter role, nudging mood upward over the following hour or so, which is probably why comfort food feels less like a spike and more like a settling.

Meanwhile cortisol, the stress hormone, actually drops after eating carb-heavy comfort food — which is the biological reason it works, even if it's not a long-term fix.

  • Dopamine

    Reward + anticipation, peaks before the first bite

  • Serotonin

    Slower mood lift, builds over the following hour

  • Cortisol

    Drops after eating, easing short-term stress
