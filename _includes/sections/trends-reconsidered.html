Comfort
food,
reconsidered

The point was never to cut comfort food out. It's to see what happens when the same feeling gets rebuilt with better ingredients.

{% include picture.html img="cauliflower-mac" class="figure--card split__img" sizes="(min-width: 900px) 34vw, 92vw" %}

A growing wave of home cooks and chefs are rebuilding classic comfort dishes with nutrient-dense swaps — cauliflower mac and cheese, lentil-based "meat" sauces, yogurt-based creamy bases.

The psychology stays intact even when the ingredients change, since warmth, creaminess, and familiarity are about texture and association, not specifically sugar or fat.

Mindful eating research backs this up: slowing down and actually noticing a meal can deliver a similar calming effect to the meal itself, regardless of what's on the plate.

It's less about restriction and more about redesigning comfort so it doesn't always come with a guilt tax attached.
