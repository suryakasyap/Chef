Turns out our cravings are a few hundred thousand years out of date.

For most of human history, food was unreliable. A brain wired to crave energy-dense food — heavy in sugar and fat — was simply more likely to survive the next famine.

That wiring hasn't updated, even though food scarcity mostly has. We're still biologically rewarded for choosing the fries over the salad, regardless of what our fridge actually looks like.

It's not a willpower problem so much as a mismatch — an ancient survival instinct running in a world it wasn't built for.

Which honestly took some pressure off, once we understood it. Reaching for the energy-dense option isn't a personal failing, it's a few hundred thousand years of programming doing exactly what it's supposed to.

"Your cravings aren't broken. They're just running on outdated software."
